# Sufra menu plan

The dishes the recipe library is written from, one recipe file per chunk (`src/data/recipes/<chunk>.ts`). The core is Kurdish cooking and its Iraqi, Turkish, Persian and Levantine neighbours; healthy dishes from other kitchens live in the `*-international` chunks, and easy desserts are planned as snacks. The machine-readable source is `docs/menu.json`; `node scripts/check-menu.mjs` checks it against the balance rules. Each recipe author writes exactly the dishes listed for their chunk, keeping the id, name, native name, cuisine, slots and the diet/allergen profile shown here (flags are derived from ingredients, so the recipe must actually be gluten-free, dairy-free and so on where the plan says so).

Legend: **GF** gluten-free, **DF** dairy-free, **NF** nut-free (peanuts count as nuts), **EF** egg-free, **Quick** 30 minutes or less from start to table (prep + cook; an overnight soak or a 40-minute pot of brown rice makes a dish not quick). ✓ = yes, · = no. Slots: B breakfast, L lunch, D dinner, S snack.

Health limits every recipe must meet at one serving: breakfast 250-650 kcal, ≥10 g protein, ≥3 g fiber, ≤700 mg sodium; lunch/dinner 350-800 kcal, ≥15 g protein, ≥4 g fiber, ≤900 mg sodium; snacks 80-350 kcal, ≤350 mg sodium; saturated fat ≤12% of energy everywhere. Halal only: no pork, alcohol (mirin, cooking wine, wine or sherry vinegar, vanilla extract), gelatin or blood.

## At a glance

- 137 dishes, 47 of them Kurdish or Iraqi and 53 more Turkish, Persian or Levantine; the other 37 are healthy dishes from other kitchens (Western, East, South and Southeast Asian, Latin American, Mediterranean, North African) and simple desserts.
- 58 vegan, 30 vegetarian, 13 pescatarian, 36 with meat or poultry.
- Main protein across the 79 lunch and dinner dishes: 27 legumes (including edamame), 16 chicken, 16 lamb or beef, 13 fish, 5 eggs or dairy, 1 tofu, and 1 wheat-based (kallane).
- Every lunch dish also lists dinner. Iraqi red lentil soup, ezogelin soup, Persian kuku sabzi and Kurdish kallane are breakfasts too.
- Desserts are planned in the snack slot: Kurdish rice pudding, kleicha, hoşaf, yogurt with honey, baked apples, oranges with pomegranate, plus easy oat, date and yogurt sweets, a tropical fruit salad, frozen yogurt bark, banana nice cream and dark-chocolate strawberries.

## Notes for recipe authors

These choices are what make the dishes pass the health gate; keep them.

- **Sodium.** Canned fava beans (about 450 mg per 100 g) are too salty: bagilla starts from dried beans. Iraqi dolma uses fresh or frozen vine leaves, never brined jarred ones. Muhammara uses fresh peppers charred at home, not jarred. Canned chickpeas are the low-sodium kind everywhere, as are the kidney beans in ghormeh sabzi. Salt is measured and kept low: every recipe stays at or below 1,500 mg of sodium per 1,000 kcal and the median recipe in each slot at or below 1,000 mg (checked in `src/data/data.test.ts`), because the planner scales portions up to 2x and a day is judged per kcal. Lemon, sumac, herbs and spices do the seasoning; mastaw gets no added salt, and shalgham counts its turnips as boiled and drained, since the cooking water they leach their sodium into is poured away.
- **Saturated fat.** Low-fat and 2% yogurt is already over 12% of energy from saturated fat on its own, and so are eggs (about 20%). Yogurt-led dishes (çılbır, mücver, jajik, mastaw, the parfait, the Kurdish breakfast plate) use nonfat yogurt. Egg-led dishes carry a low-fat partner: lavash with the kuku. Mince is lean: 95% lean beef in makhlama and kofta, and minced lean lamb leg (not ready-minced lamb) in dolma and qeema. The Kurdish breakfast plate keeps its white cheese to about 15 g.
- **Hidden alcohol.** Mustard is plain yellow mustard (Dijon is often made with alcohol). Desserts get their scent from cardamom, cinnamon and rosewater, never vanilla extract. Keep haram words out of recipe text entirely, even in phrases like "no rice ___", because the validator rejects the words themselves.
